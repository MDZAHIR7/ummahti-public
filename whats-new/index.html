Release history

What’s new

Every Ummahti release and what it added, newest first. The same history is in the app, under Settings, and it reads offline.

The next chapter of Ummahti Quran

1.5.0

New ways to keep the Qur’an close every day, and many smaller refinements.

Ask Guide about more topics

Guide now covers wudu, the words after the adhan and after each prayer, and everyday du’as. It also covers family ties and longing for a child. Its Ramadan, travel, illness and loss pages are fuller. An answer opens on the verse itself, under your own words. Each topic carries the du’as from the library that belong to it.

Plan a khatm

Choose a date or a length, and Ummahti divides the Qur’an into daily readings. Home shows today’s portion and one way into it. Nothing is marked read unless you say so.

Practise what you’ve memorised

Keep a record of what you know or are learning, from one ayah to the whole Quran. See it by surah or by juz, and practise any passage in one tap. Hide an ayah and take a hint when a word will not come.

Read two Mushaf pages side by side

On an open book style foldable, or a tablet turned sideways, read two Mushaf pages side by side. They are set line for line as in the Madinah Mushaf, and you turn them like a book. Qur’an Pages opens by itself on these wide screens. You can change it in the reading view menu.

Listen with no connection

Download surahs in your chosen reciter’s voice and listen with no connection at all. See what is stored and how much room it takes. Remove any of it whenever you like.

Pass On the Light

Invite one person to the Qur’an in a couple of taps. Choose where they start: install Ummahti, see the website first, or listen on YouTube. Then send a card that carries its own address, so it still works where a caption is lost.

A library of du'as

Guide’s du’as are now a library on eleven shelves: hardship, healing, protection, family, travel, sleep and every ordinary day. Every du’a names its source, and every narration names its grading.

Recite to add

Recite a passage you remember, and My Hifz finds it with its ayahs and text, ready to save. If you close it before you save, the ayahs it found wait for you.

Recite in Memorise and Practice

In Memorise and Practice, tap Recite and recite the hidden ayah from memory. Ummahti follows the words you recite against the ayah, on your phone. It marks places to check, with a note under the ayah. It does not check pronunciation, vowels or tajwid. It never marks an ayah memorised or changes your revision schedule.

Recite Along on Qur’an Pages

On an open book style foldable, tap Recite Along at the edge of the printed page and recite aloud. The printed page marks the words it follows. Tap the control again to stop. It does not check pronunciation, vowels or tajwid.

Recitation recognition, on your phone

Recite to add, Recite in Memorise and Practice, and Recite Along share one recitation pack. You download it once, about 107 MB, from Ummahti’s public release on GitHub. It runs on your phone. The microphone opens only when you tap. Your voice is never recorded, saved or uploaded. Arrange Home the way you read

Move Home cards, put them away, or pair a card with the one beside it. My Khatm no longer holds a fixed place. Continue Reading always stays first. Press and hold a card to arrange it, and Undo takes back any step.

Home opens like a book on a foldable

Open a book style foldable and Home uses both halves. The Qur’an waits open at the verse where you stopped, with Resume under your thumb. The rest of Home sits beside it, in your own order. The navigation stands on the outer edge, and Settings can move it to the other side.

Saved, like the index of a Mushaf

Saved now lists each surah once, with its ayahs beneath it. Select from one tray at your thumb. Deleted bookmarks come back with Undo.

Open any juz, by the name you call it

The reading picker now has a Juz / Para list beside the surahs. Browse all thirty, see where each one begins and ends, and open it there. Search finds them by number or by the para name you were taught. Juz 30, Para 30 and Amma all reach the same place. Each surah now shows its juz.

A new English transliteration

English transliteration now comes from MuslimNet’s edition, carried inside the app. Its underlined and bold letters show how each word is said. The key sits under the language list.

Your own ayahs on the widget

Choose ayahs from Saved for your home screen widget. Show them in turn, mix them with the daily ayah, or keep one until you change it.

Repeat a verse, in Hifz too

Tap the loop button on the player, and what plays repeats each time it ends. It works for a single verse or a passage in Hifz. The player shows what repeats and how many times. Tap the loop button again to stop.

Practise from memory with hints

Memorise and Practice now keep the ayah in place. Hide it, take a hint at the next word, and practise the join with the ayah before. Practise any surah, juz or range without recording it first. When a practice ends, one step takes you back to the ayahs where you took a hint.

Find a surah by the name you know it by

Search now finds each surah by its name in every language Ummahti speaks, such as Bakara for Surah 2. Every spelling that worked before still works.

Ummahti fits the screen you are on

On a tablet or a phone turned sideways, the reading keeps a comfortable column. The navigation stays centred at the foot of the screen. Settings opens each page beside its list. Saved verses and downloads fill the width.

Carry on reading from anywhere

Add a Continue reading tile to the panel you pull down from the top of the screen. One tap opens Ummahti where you left off. You can add it from Settings.

Slide the moon to switch tabs

Hold the selected moon, slide to another tab, then release to open it.

Small changes, easier days.

  • Type a reference such as 2:255 into the surah picker and it offers to open that verse.
  • The widget has a new look. It shows the ayah in your reader’s typeface, with its verse sign, on lit glass, at every size.
  • Settings has a search box, so any option is a few letters away.
  • A shared verse now names its translator, and you can choose whether to include the translation.
  • The play button shows when recitation is still loading, and offers to try again if it cannot start.
  • Changing the app’s language no longer changes the translation you read.
  • Anything you can swipe away can now also be removed with TalkBack, Switch Access or a keyboard.
  • The navigation bar keeps every label whole at the largest text size.
  • If Help & FAQ doesn’t answer your question, you can send it to us straight from there.
  • Reflection of the Moment now takes turns between a verse and a hadith, and draws on more of both. It names who graded each narration from Jamiʽ at Tirmidhi.

1.4.0

Ummahti Guide

Describe what you are facing in your own words. Guide answers from the Qur’an and authentic Sunnah, and every answer names the exact source you can open and read for yourself. It works fully offline, and nothing you type leaves your phone.

Du’as inside Guide

A curated set of Qur’anic and authentic Prophetic supplications, each with a reading aid, its meaning, its exact reference, and when it is authentically said. No invented repetition counts, and no promised outcomes.

Read, Save and Reflect

Every verse Guide shows you opens in the reader, saves to your bookmarks, or takes a private reflection, through the same reader, the same Saved and the same reflections you already use. Come back and Guide is still on the answer you left.

When you need support

Guide now tells an ordinary heavy week apart from an emergency, and answers each differently: practical, sourced guidance for the first, and a short, direct instruction to reach a person for the second. It is always one tap from Guide’s landing.

Reading Themes and Heritage

Twelve reading themes, each with a live preview and an honest note on where its pattern came from: derived from a tradition, broadly inspired by one, or Ummahti’s own. The theme picker in Settings works exactly as it always did; this sits beside it, for when you want to look before you choose.

Three of the twelve are new. Classic Ink is a monochrome reading environment: white paper, black ink and nothing else. Shiraz Dawn is cool stone with morning light arriving through coloured glass at the margins. Jali Moon is a pierced stone screen at night, at the lowest light the app emits. No reader’s chosen theme moves, and two accents were deepened so that labels and controls clear their contrast floor.

Mushaf al-Madinah

The seventh calligraphy style, set in the King Fahd Complex’s own face over the Complex’s own spelling of the text. A face and the orthography it was cut for are one decision rather than two, so Ummahti ships them as a pair.

Eleven more reciters

Thirty-eight voices now, murattal and mujawwad. Tap any verse to hear it, and your reading place stays exactly where you left it.

Video from the Ummahti channel

Previewed inside the app rather than sending you out to a browser.

Why “Ummahti”?

A new page under About: the name, and the narration it comes from, with its collection, reference and chapter. It reads offline. The short version is on the home page.

1.3.0

Sacred Times

Jumu’ah, Ramadan and the last ten nights, both Eids, Arafah, Ashura and the sacred months each change the light of the app and open onto an authored guide: what the time means, what the Prophet ﷺ and the Companions did, what you can do now, and the source behind every line.

A reflection library

Home draws from three hundred written reflections, each one anchored to a single exact ayah or to one graded narration. The source sits under every reflection, and tapping it opens it.

Eight more reciters

Yasser Al-Dosari, Nasser Al-Qatami, Khalid Al-Jalil, Idris Abkar, Fares Abbad, Saad Al-Ghamdi, Bandar Balila and Mustafa Ismail, which brought the choice to twenty-seven voices at the time.

Reading that scrolls itself

The page scrolls at a pace you set, in Verse Stream and Continuous Mushaf. Touch the page to pause; your pace is remembered.

Sleep timer

Set the recitation to fade out after a while, or at the end of the current surah.

Resize your Home cards

Press and hold any card on Home, then drag its handles. Corners resize continuously, each card adapts its layout, and the page reflows as you go.

Your bookmarks, arranged your way

Group saved verses into collections you name yourself. A verse can sit in as many as you like, and nothing is filed for you.

One reading place, when you want it

Keep Ayah on Unlock separate, share its place with your main reading, or move either journey to an exact ayah you choose.

And the rest

  • A searchable surah picker, by name or number, showing where each surah was revealed.
  • A quiet mark when you finish a juz, shown only after you have genuinely read through it.
  • Shared verse cards drawn in the app’s own Qur’an typeface.
  • The screen stays awake while you are actively reading, and returns to normal when you leave the reader.
  • Offline Help and FAQ, searchable without a connection.
